This elegant building on Curetes Street is commonly known as the Temple of Hadrian. The name does not mean its precise function is beyond debate: researchers have studied its role in the imperial cult and its relationship to the baths behind it. Its arch, columns and plant motifs draw attention. Dating to the second century, the façade was re-erected in the twentieth century using excavated fragments. The originals of the reliefs displayed at the front are preserved in the Ephesus Museum; copies are shown here. This small monument offers rich details for understanding the architecture of Ephesus.
