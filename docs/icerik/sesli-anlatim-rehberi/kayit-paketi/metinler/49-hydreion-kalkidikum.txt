At the western end of Curetes Street, right beside the Monument of Memmius, stands a fountain house with a semicircular plan. Known in excavation reports as the Hydreion, this nymphaeum was a visible node in the city's water network, with its niche and two basins facing the street. Four pedestals in front of the niche carried statues of the Tetrarchs, which shows the building was still in use in the late third century CE. The date of its first phase is disputed: some sources point to the Augustan period, others to the late second century CE. The remains now lie below street level, so they are hard to make out without looking closely.
