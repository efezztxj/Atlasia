The monument known as the Tomb of Saint Luke is a circular structure near the southern entrance area of Ephesus. Despite its name, its identification as Saint Luke's tomb is not established. According to research by Austrian archaeologist Andreas Pülz, it was originally built as a Roman fountain and adapted for use as a church in the fifth century. Its transformation illustrates how ancient buildings were reused in later periods. The map marker represents the remains, not a visitor entrance.
