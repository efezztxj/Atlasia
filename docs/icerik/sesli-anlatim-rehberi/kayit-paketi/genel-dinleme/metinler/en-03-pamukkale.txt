At Pamukkale the eye finds white first, then the ear catches the water. The spring is heavy with lime; for centuries it ran down the slope, left calcium carbonate behind and built these terraces step by step. The water is still warm, around thirty-five degrees. You have to take your shoes off on the terraces; the stone is slippery and hard, and bare feet grip better. Ancient Hierapolis stands on the same slope above: its theatre, its necropolis and its thermal pool all describe the same relationship with the water. As the sun goes down the white surface turns pink, and that is also when the crowds thin out.
